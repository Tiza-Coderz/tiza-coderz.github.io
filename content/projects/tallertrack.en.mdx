## The problem

In a typical Havana repair shop, orders are written in a notebook or a WhatsApp group. That causes problems that cost money and customers:

- **"Is my phone ready?"**: the owner answers the same question all day.
- **Complaints** about scratches or accessories because nobody recorded how the device came in.
- **Parts that disappear**: nobody knows how many screens are left or which device they went into.
- **Disputed warranties**: no quick way to know when and who repaired an IMEI.
- **Confusing prices** when the USD rate changes every week.

## The solution

**TallerTrack** is a web app that runs on the technician's phone on slow data and installs like an app:

- **Device intake**: customer, IMEI, fault, physical condition, accessories, photos, password or pattern (encrypted), quote in USD with its CUP equivalent and promised date.
- **PDF ticket** in 80 mm (thermal printer) or A4, with a **QR code to the tracking page**.
- **Status workflow** from "Received" to "Delivered", with history, internal notes and customer-visible notes.
- **WhatsApp notification** with the message pre-written for each status and the total in USD and CUP.
- **Public tracking** without login and without sensitive data.
- **Parts inventory** with automatic deduction when used, low-stock alerts and movement history.
- **Warranty** with IMEI search and order reopening.
- **Reports** on orders, monthly revenue, profit per part and technician performance.

## The result

The shop **stops living on the phone**: customers scan the QR on their ticket and see how the repair is going. Every device is documented from the moment it arrives, every part knows where it was used and the owner sees what makes money. The key rules (status changes, stock, warranty) are enforced in the database, so they don't depend on someone remembering.
